Treating cancer
as a physics problem

I build tools that ask not what a tumor looks like, but where it is — how stable it is in its current state, how close it is to tipping into a different one, and whether that distance can be measured before treatment begins rather than after it fails.

Current work
oncoAttractor
Reconstructs the transcriptional attractor landscape of tumors from bulk RNA-seq data. Computes quasi-potential geometry — basin depth, basin width, saddle point proximity — to predict treatment resistance and clinical outcome. The saddle point proximity score is the core result: a tumor sitting close to the boundary between a sensitive and a resistant attractor basin, before a single dose of treatment, should carry that instability in its gene expression signature. Validated across TCGA cancer types. R package.
